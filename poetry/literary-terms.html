🔍 Literary Terms

Figures of speech and sound devices used in poetry.

🎭 Figures of Speech
Imaginative Language
1. Personification

Giving human characteristics to inanimate objects or things.

Example: "The crying moon" — the poet personifies the moon as a crying human being.

2. Simile

A clear and direct comparison between two things that are different using words such as "like" or "as".

Example: "The clouds looked like cotton candy."

3. Metaphor

An indirect comparison between two unlike things that have something in common.

Example: "Her voice is a nice song."

4. Contrast

The use of opposite words to make the meaning clear.

Example: "I looked up and down."

5. Hyperbole

An extreme exaggeration used to make a point.

Example: "My grandmother is as old as the hills."

🔊 Sound Devices
Rhythm & Sound
1. Rhyme Scheme

The pattern of rhyming words at the end of each line. Presented by giving the same letter of the alphabet to each similar sound.

In winter, I get up at night (a)
And dress by yellow candle light. (a)
In summer, quite the other way (b)
I have to go to bed by day. (b)

Rhyme scheme: aabb

2. Alliteration

The repetition of the same sounds at the beginning of words in a line.

Example: "I am busy," said the sea. (repetition of /s/ sound)

3. Repetition

The repetition of a sound, word or phrase for emphasis (to strengthen the idea or to make it clear).

Inside the house I get ready
Inside the can I go to school
Inside the school I wait for the bell to ring
