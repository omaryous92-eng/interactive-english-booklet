📊 Comparison: Simple vs Continuous

✅ Present Perfect Simple
Form: have/has + past participle
  • Result — past action with present result
  • Experience — life experiences (ever, never)
  • Unfinished — actions that continue (for, since)
  • Recent — recent events (just, already, yet)
"I've just had dinner."
"Have you ever been to Europe?"
"He has lived here since 2010."
🔄 Present Perfect Continuous
Form: have/has + been + verb-ing
  • Duration — started in the past and continues to now
  • Emphasis — on how long the action has been happening
  • Repeated — actions repeated up to now
"I have been working since 6 a.m."
"She has been studying for hours."
"They have been playing tennis all afternoon."
🧭 Have been to vs Have gone to
✅ Have been to

Used to talk about experiences — places one has travelled to.

"He has been to Iraq, Russia and North Korea."
⚡ Meaning: He went there and came back.
❌ Have gone to

Used to say someone has gone somewhere and is still there.

"She has gone to bed."
⚡ Meaning: She went to bed and is still there.
⏰ Signal Words
Quick Reference
Word Meaning Example
just A very short time ago "I've just had dinner."
already Before now / earlier than expected "It has already started."
yet Before now / until now (negative/questions) "He hasn't arrived yet."
for Duration (period of time) "I've lived here for 10 years."
since Specific point in time "I've lived here since 2010."
ever / never General experiences "Have you ever been to Paris?"
📌 Quick Reference Card
Present Perfect Simple
  • 📌 Result of past action
  • ⭐ Life experiences
  • 🔄 Unfinished actions (for/since)
  • 📰 Recent news (just/yet/already)
Present Perfect Continuous
  • ⏳ Duration of an action
  • ⚡ Emphasis on how long
  • 🔁 Repeated actions
  • 🔥 Cause of present state
