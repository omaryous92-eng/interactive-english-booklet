⏳ Present Perfect Simple & Continuous

Forms, usage, signal words, and the difference between been and gone.

✅ Present Perfect Simple
Result / Experience
Form: have/has + past participle
I have spoken / She has worked

Usage

  • Past action with present result
  • Life experiences (ever, never)
  • Unfinished actions (for, since)
  • Recent events (just, already, yet)

Examples

  • I've just had dinner.
  • Have you ever been to Europe?
  • He has lived here since 2010.
  • She hasn't arrived yet.
🔄 Present Perfect Continuous
Duration / Ongoing
Form: have/has + been + verb-ing
I have been speaking / She has been working

Usage

  • Actions that started in the past and continue to now
  • Emphasis on duration
  • Repeated actions up to now

Examples

  • I have been working since 6 a.m.
  • She has been studying for hours.
  • They have been playing tennis all afternoon.
🧭 Have been to vs Have gone to
  • Have been to – to talk about experiences / places visited (e.g., He has been to Iraq.)
  • Have gone to – to say someone has gone and is still there (e.g., She has gone to bed.)
⏰ Signal Words
Word Meaning Example
justA very short time agoI've just had dinner.
alreadyBefore now / earlier than expectedIt has already started.
yetBefore now / until now (negative/questions)He hasn't arrived yet.
forDuration (period of time)I've lived here for 10 years.
sinceSpecific point in timeI've lived here since 2010.
ever/neverGeneral experiencesHave you ever been to Paris?
