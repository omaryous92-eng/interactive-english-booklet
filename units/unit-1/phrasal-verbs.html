🔗 Phrasal Verbs & Idioms

Common expressions from the booklet – tap to flip and learn.

💡 Idioms

jump for joy
be extremely happy
"I would jump for joy if I passed all my exams!"
take for granted
believe that sth is true without having any evidence
"Don't take your parents' support for granted, some kids don't have that."
drive crazy
make sb very angry
"That sound is really starting to drive me crazy!"
get stuck
be unable to move or continue
"I couldn't move forward in traffic because I got stuck in the jam."
push your way
push the people around you in order to get somewhere
"The plane was so full I had to push my way through the crowd to get on."
on the edge of our seats
thrilled, very excited
"We were all on the edge of our seats during the final scene of the film."
get under my skin
make sb upset, annoy or irritate
"That sound really gets under my skin – I can't stand it anymore!"
lost for words
not know what to say because you are shocked or surprised
"I was completely lost for words after I tripped in front of the whole school."
in agony
feeling a lot of pain
"He gripped his arm and cried out in agony after falling off the bike."
with regret
a feeling of sadness about sth unpleasant you have to do or say
"She looked back at the choice she made, filled with regret."
burst into tears
suddenly start to cry
"After hearing the bad news, she couldn't hold it in and burst into tears."
burst out laughing
suddenly start to laugh
"The joke was so funny I burst out laughing."

🔁 Phrasal Verbs

gone off
stop liking or being interested in sb
"I've completely gone off her after the way she treated me."
cheered up
make (someone) happier
"A call from her best friend really cheered her up after the tough day."
chill out
relax without letting anything upset you
"After all that stress, you really need to chill out and relax."
hit it off
become friends with sb immediately
"They immediately hit it off over their shared love of books."
fall out
argue with sb and stop being friends with them
"They used to be inseparable, but they suddenly fell out last week."
get down
make sb feel unhappy
"The repetitive tapping noise from the window began to get me down."
stand up
not come to meet someone you had arranged to meet
"I waited for an hour, but she never arrived—she completely stood me up!"
