📖 Vocabulary 1

upset (v)
make sb feel worried and/or angry
"The bad news upset her and she couldn't stop thinking about it."
behaviour (n)
the way that sb acts
"His behaviour in class is very polite and respectful."
sight (n)
sth that you see
"The sight of the mountains at sunrise was amazing."
wonder (v)
ask yourself; want to know
"I wonder if it will rain tomorrow."
bother (v)
to make sb angry or upset or worried
"The loud noise bothered me while I was studying."
context (n)
the situation in which sth happens and which can explain it
"You need to read the sentence in context to understand it."
supply (n)
an amount of sth that is available and people can use
"The village has a limited supply of clean water."
public transport (n)
the system of buses, trains, etc. that can be used by the public in a specific place
"I use public transport to go to school every day."
interruption (n)
the act of stopping sth for a short period of time
"There was an interruption during the exam because of a fire alarm."
bug (v)
annoy, irritate
"His constant talking really bugs me."
decade (n)
a period of ten years
"She has lived in Cairo for over a decade."
research (n)
the study of sth in order to discover new information
"The students did research on climate change."
condition (n)
a disease or medical problem
"He has a heart condition that needs regular treatment."
misophonia (n)
a medical condition in which certain sounds cause a strong negative reaction
"Her misophonia makes it hard for her to eat in noisy places."
respond (v)
react to sth by saying or doing sth
"She didn't respond to my message immediately."
yawn (v)
open your mouth wide and take in a lot of air, when you are tired or sleepy
"I couldn't stop yawning during the long lecture."
chew (v)
bite food into small pieces with your teeth so that you can eat it
"You should chew your food slowly before swallowing."
suffer (v)
experience pain
"Many people suffer from allergies in spring."
benefit (n)
a good effect of sth
"One benefit of exercise is better health."
prevent (v)
stop sb from doing sth or stop sth from happening
"Wearing a seatbelt can prevent serious injuries."
amazement (n)
extreme surprise
"To my amazement, I got full marks on the test."
anxiety (n)
worry or nervousness about sth that is happening or will happen
"She felt anxiety before her final exam."
despair (n)
the feeling that there is no hope and nothing will get better
"He was in despair after failing the competition."
embarrassment (n)
a feeling of shame and/or shyness
"His embarrassment was clear when he fell in front of everyone."
frustration (n)
the state of feeling bad because you cannot achieve what you want
"I felt frustration when the internet stopped working."
guilt (n)
a feeling of unhappiness you get because you have done sth wrong
"She felt guilt after lying to her friend."
joy (n)
a feeling of happiness
"The children shouted with joy when they heard the news."
relief (n)
a feeling of happiness because sth bad has not happened or has ended
"I felt relief after finishing the difficult test."
sympathy (n)
the understanding that you show when sb else is suffering
"I felt sympathy for the family who lost their home."

😊 Emotions & Personality

harmful
causing damage or injury
"The chemicals in this product are very harmful to the environment."
irritating
making you feel angry
"That mosquito noise was really irritating while I was trying to study."
furious
very angry
"He was furious after hearing the news that he lost his job."
annoying
make sb angry
"His constant talking really annoys me."
awful
very bad and unpleasant
"The food was awful and we couldn't eat it."
packed
completely full (Syn: crowded)
"This restaurant is always packed on weekends."
overjoyed
extremely happy
"She was overjoyed after hearing the good news."
distracted
confused because you are thinking of sth else
"I was too distracted to concentrate during the test."
unpleasant
not enjoyable (Opp: pleasant)
"The weather was unpleasant and we decided to stay indoors."
unpredictable
likely to change unexpectedly (Opp: predictable)
"His behavior is unpredictable and difficult to understand."
delayed
happening later than expected (Opp: punctual)
"The train was delayed due to bad weather conditions."
tolerable
just satisfactory; not too good, not bad
"The food was tolerable, but nothing special."
aggressive
angry and violent
"She acted so aggressive that no one wanted to sit near her."
calm
peaceful and without worry
"He remained calm even when others were angry."
arrogant
behaving as if you were better and/or knew more than everyone else
"Her arrogant attitude made her classmates avoid her."
bad-tempered
can easily become angry or annoyed
"He has a very strong and bad-tempered personality."
loyal
willing to support sb even at difficult times
"She is loyal to her friends and always helps them."
mature
behaving like an adult
"She deals with problems in a very mature way for her age."
mean
unkind and unpleasant
"His mean remarks made everyone feel hurt."
stubborn
unwilling to listen to others or change what you think
"That child was extremely stubborn and refused to listen to anyone."
sensitive
easily upset
"He is very sensitive and gets upset easily."
childish
behaving like a child
"His childish behavior annoyed everyone in the room."
optimistic
believing that good things will happen in the future
"Even though things were hard, he remained optimistic."
reliable
that can be trusted
"She is reliable and always keeps her word."

🔹 Adverbs

extremely
very
"I felt extremely disappointed when I saw my test result."
seriously
very or severely
"He always speaks seriously about important issues."
closely
carefully, paying attention to details
"I worked closely with my teacher to improve my grades."
constantly
all the time or regularly (Syn: continually)
"The boy shouted constantly for five minutes."
noisily
loudly
"I couldn't focus with all that noisily talking in the background."
