📖 Past Perfect

Express the past action already finished when another past action happened.

✅ Form
Had + Past Participle
Positive: I had finished my homework.
Negative: She hadn't eaten breakfast.
Question: Had you seen the film before?
🔹 Usage
Before another past action
  • Action completed before another past action — I met them after they had divorced each other.
  • Two events in the past that are linked — I couldn't take my flight because I had forgotten my boarding pass.
  • Result of an activity in the past — I had been to London twice by the time I got a job in New York.
⏰ Time Expressions
After
After he had had his lunch, he watched TV.
Before
Before I went to the club, I had called my sister.
As soon as
As soon as she had finished, she left.
By the time
By the time we arrived, the film had started.
When
When I got home, she had already left.
Until / till
I didn't go to bed until I had finished my work.
📌 Notes
  • After and before can be followed by verb+ing form: After having his lunch, he watched TV.
  • Having + past participle = After: Having had his lunch, he watched TV.
  • Negative past simple + until/till + past perfect: I didn't watch TV until I had had my lunch.
