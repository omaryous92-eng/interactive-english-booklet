📖 Past Perfect vs Past Perfect Continuous

Express actions completed before another past action, with duration and cause — with visual illustrations.

✅ Past Perfect

Form: had + past participle

Positive
I had finished my homework.
Negative
She hadn't eaten breakfast.
Question
Had you seen the film before?
  • Action completed before another past action — I met them after they had divorced each other.
  • Two events in the past that are linked — I couldn't take my flight because I had forgotten my boarding pass.
  • Result of an activity in the past — I had been to London twice by the time I got a job in New York.
🔄 Past Perfect Continuous

Form: had been + verb-ing

Positive
I had been working for hours.
Negative
She hadn't been sleeping well.
Question
Had you been waiting long?
  • Emphasize the duration of an activity — Richard needed a holiday because he had been working hard for six months.
  • Show the cause of a past action — I had been travelling all night, so I was tired.
  • Action that started in the past and continued to another past action — Sara had been working here for two weeks when she had the accident.

📊 Comparison: Past Perfect vs Past Perfect Continuous

✅ Past Perfect
  • Completed action — before another past action
  • Result — of a past activity
  • Linked events — cause and effect
"I had finished my homework."
"I had been to London twice."
🔄 Past Perfect Continuous
  • Duration — how long an action lasted
  • Cause — of a past situation
  • Ongoing — action continued until another past action
"I had been doing my homework for 2 hours."
"I was tired because I had been working."

⏰ Time Expressions

📊 Time Expressions Table
Quick Reference
Expression Structure Example
After past perfect, past simple "After he had had his lunch, he watched the television."
As soon as past perfect, past simple "As soon as she had finished, she left."
Because past perfect, past simple "I was tired because I had been working."
Before past simple, past perfect "Before I went to the club, I had called my sister."
By the time past simple, past perfect "By the time we arrived, the film had started."
When past simple, past perfect "When I got home, she had already left."
Until / Till negative past simple, past perfect "I didn't go to bed until I had finished my work."
📝 Notes
1. After and before can be followed by verb+ing form.
After having his lunch, he watched the television.
2. Having + past participle = After
Having had his lunch, he watched the television.
3. Negative past simple + until/till + past perfect
I didn't watch the television until I had had my lunch.
