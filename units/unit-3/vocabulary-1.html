⭐ Vocabulary 1: Role Models

Tap any card to flip it and see the definition + example. Learn at your own pace!

26 Words 8 Comparisons 8 Idioms

📖 Vocabulary Words

role model (n)
a person that you admire and try to copy
"My older sister is my role model."
potential (n)
sb's ability to develop or succeed
"Every player has the potential to improve."
mission (n)
sth important you want to achieve
"The school's mission is to help students succeed."
impact (n)
a powerful effect
"Social media can have a big impact."
motivate (v)
make sb want to do sth
"My parents motivate me to do my best."
inspire (v)
give sb the confidence to do sth and idea to follow
"The scientist inspired many students."
criticise (v)
say what you think is wrong about sb/sth
"It is easy to criticise someone."
barrier (n)
sth that prevents people from doing sth
"Language can be a barrier."
expectation (n)
a strong belief about how sb should behave
"There is an expectation that students will complete their homework."
admire (v)
respect and approve of sb
"I admire people who work hard."
guide (n)
sb/sth that shows you the way
"Our teacher acted as a guide."
trail (n)
path through a forest or in the countryside
"We followed a narrow trail through the forest."
phenomenon (n)
sb/sth that is extremely successful and well known
"She became a worldwide phenomenon."
remote (adj)
far away from where most people live
"They worked in a remote village."
eldest (adj)
the oldest of three or more people in a family
"My eldest brother is studying engineering."
fetch (v)
go, get and bring back sb/sth
"Could you fetch me a bottle of water?"
crop (n)
plants that are grown in large quantities as food
"Rice is an important crop."
afford (v)
have the money or time to buy or do sth
"I cannot afford to buy a new laptop."
excel (v)
be extremely good at sth
"She continues to excel in mathematics."
equipment (n)
the necessary items for a particular purpose
"The laboratory has modern equipment."
donation (n)
money that you give to help others
"The charity received a large donation."
compete (v)
take part in a race or competition
"Athletes will compete in the tournament."
attempt (v)
try to do sth
"He will attempt to solve the problem."
ultra (prefix)
extreme or extremely
"They used an ultra-modern computer system."
right (n)
the expectation to be treated fairly
"Every child has the right to education."
sponsorship (n)
money given to support an activity
"The event was possible because of the company's sponsorship."

📌 What is the difference?

Older vs Elder

Older (adj.)

Used for people and things

"My car is older than yours."

Elder (adj.)

Used only for people (family members)

"My elder sister is a doctor."

Cope vs Overcome

Cope (v)

Still experience problem but manage to work around it

"She coped with the pressure."

Overcome (v)

Deal with successfully (problem mostly solved)

"She overcame her fear."

Gossip vs Rumour

Gossip (n)

Conversation about other people's private lives, usually unkind or untrue

"She was spreading gossip."

Rumour (n)

A story or piece of news that quickly spreads

"There is a rumour that he's leaving."

Disgrace vs Scandal

Disgrace (n)

Loss of people's respect because of unacceptable behaviour

"His actions brought disgrace."

Scandal (n)

An action that causes the public to feel shock

"The politician was involved in a scandal."

Breakthrough vs Comeback

Breakthrough (n)

An important discovery that helps solve a problem

"Scientists made a breakthrough."

Comeback (n)

The return to fame or power of sb who was famous before

"The actor made a comeback."

💡 Idioms

follow in footsteps
do the same thing as sb else did before
"He followed in his father's footsteps."
out of nowhere
suddenly and surprisingly
"She appeared out of nowhere."
seize the opportunity
make use of a chance as soon as it appears
"He seized the opportunity to work abroad."
the secret of success
the way in which sb has achieved success
"Hard work is the secret of her success."
open doors
provide opportunities for sb
"Her degree opened doors for her."
claim to fame
the reason why sb might say they are famous
"Her claim to fame was winning a singing competition."
never get anywhere
make no progress or have no success
"If you don't work hard, you'll never get anywhere."
household name
a famous person that almost everyone knows
"He became a household name."
