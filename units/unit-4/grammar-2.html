📖 Countable & Uncountable Nouns

Nouns that can be counted, nouns that can't, and how to use quantifiers.

✅ Countable Nouns
Can be counted
  • Can take singular or plural verbs: There is a book. Those houses are big.
  • Go with a/an/the/my, etc. in the singular: She bought an umbrella.
  • Can be used alone with some/any/many/few in the plural: I love carrots.
❌ Uncountable Nouns
Cannot be counted
  • Always take singular verbs: There is some water.
  • Do not go with a/an/two, etc.: Can you hear music?
  • Can be used alone or with some/any/much/little, etc.: There is little information.
  • Can use a/an, one/two with coffee, tea... when talking about a cup/glass/bottle: Can I have two teas?
🔄 Nouns that can be Countable and Uncountable (with change of meaning)
hair – countable: two hairs; uncountable: I don't have much hair.
light – countable: two lights; uncountable: too much light.
noise – countable: many noises; uncountable: so much noise.
paper – countable: a paper (newspaper); uncountable: some paper.
room – countable: seven rooms; uncountable: Is there room for me?
time – countable: many times; uncountable: Have you got time?
work – countable: Shakespeare's works; uncountable: I need work.
📊 Quantifiers
How many? / How much?
Countable Both Uncountable
many, a few, few, several, a number ofsome, any, a lot of, lots of, plenty of, enough, no/none, more/mostmuch, a little, little, a bit of, a great deal of, a large amount of
Note: few and little have negative meaning (not enough), while a few and a little have positive meaning (some, but enough).
🧮 Counting Uncountable Nouns

Use units like: a bottle of, a grain of, a glass of, a loaf of, a piece of, a kilo of, a gram of.

  • a glass of water
  • a piece of advice
  • a loaf of bread
