🎯 Themes & Symbols

Explore the deeper meaning behind Dickens' masterpiece.
Resurrection, sacrifice, revolution, and the symbols that bring them to life.

📌 Major Themes

Hover over each card to learn more about the central ideas of the novel

🌱
Resurrection

Dr. Manette is "recalled to life" after 18 years in prison. Charles Darnay escapes the guillotine twice. Sydney Carton undergoes spiritual resurrection before his death, finding purpose and redemption through sacrifice.

Rebirth Redemption Hope
🕊️
Self-Sacrifice

Villains are defined by selfish cruelty (the Evrémonde brothers, Madame Defarge). Heroes are defined by their willingness to sacrifice themselves for others — Sydney Carton, Dr. Manette, and Miss Pross.

Selflessness Heroism Love
⚔️
Revolution & Retribution

Dickens portrays the Reign of Terror as the inevitable outcome of years of tyranny by the French aristocracy. The novel warns against the dangers of mob rule, vengeance, and the cycle of violence.

Justice Vengeance Tyranny
⚖️
Justice & Injustice

Dickens criticizes both the corrupt French aristocracy and the brutal revolutionaries. Dr. Manette's wrongful imprisonment and the misuse of the guillotine show how systems of justice can become tools of oppression.

Fairness Oppression Law
❤️
Love & Loyalty

Lucie Manette's devotion to her father and husband represents healing and hope. Loyalty is also seen in the friendship between Mr. Lorry and the Manettes, and Miss Pross's fierce protection of Lucie.

Devotion Family Friendship

🔮 Symbols

Discover the hidden meanings behind the objects and images in the novel

🍷
The Broken Wine Cask

Early in the novel, a cask of wine breaks in the street and the poor rush to drink from it. The wine represents both blood and hunger — foreshadowing the bloodshed of the revolution.

💡 Symbolizes: Bloodshed & Desperation
⚔️
The Guillotine

Intended as a fair way to execute criminals, it becomes a tool of mass murder during the Revolution. It represents the loss of moral direction and the terrifying consequences of mob rule.

💡 Symbolizes: Justice Turned to Horror
🧶
Madame Defarge's Knitting

She knits the names of those condemned to death. Her knitting symbolizes fate and vengeance — reflecting how personal hatred can fuel political violence.

💡 Symbolizes: Fate & Vengeance
🌑
Shadows & Darkness

Dickens uses images of darkness and shadows to suggest secrecy, fear, and danger. They often represent the looming threat of violence or emotional torment.

💡 Symbolizes: Fear & Danger
👣
Footsteps

Footsteps are used to represent the future drawing near. Lucie often imagines footsteps echoing around her home, foreshadowing the arrival of the revolution.

💡 Symbolizes: The Approaching Future
